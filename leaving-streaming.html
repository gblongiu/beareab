A note on distribution

Why this music lives here

beareab and ucygrx are leaving subscription services. The complete catalog will remain here, direct and free.

On this page

The music is leaving platforms, not listeners. Its home is moving closer to the people who hear it.

A deliberate withdrawal

Keeping the catalog on subscription services requires a paid distributor. Cost is only part of the decision. I do not want access to the work to depend on rented platforms or a royalty system structured around stream-share pools, rights holders, and intermediaries.

Spotify neither pays artists directly nor assigns a fixed rate to each stream. It distributes royalties to rights holders according to their share of total streams, and excludes recordings with fewer than 1,000 annual streams from its recorded-music royalty pool.

That money and energy belong in a direct archive.

Where I draw the line

Daniel Ek, Spotify’s founder and executive chair, is also a founder and principal investor in Prima Materia and co-chairman of Helsing. Helsing develops military AI systems, including the HX-2 strike drone. Prima Materia states that it operates independently of Spotify. Even with that distinction, the leadership relationship crosses a line for me.

Synthetic music

I do not want these recordings hosted by services that distribute synthetic music or build paid products around generative music. Spotify has announced a paid generative-AI tool for covers and remixes. Apple Music has editorially presented an openly AI-generated project in its catalog.

The services are not identical. The boundary is mine: I choose where this work lives.

An archive, not an absence

The aim is not scarcity. It is a more honest home for the work.

Every beareab and ucygrx release will remain here, free to stream and download in FLAC, MP3 320, and MP3 128. There is no subscription, gate, or obligation to contribute. The work is here to be heard and kept.
